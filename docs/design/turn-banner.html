What the map has for this junction

  • End of the ramp: three lanes, not four. The map has left, left-or-straight, and right. You described left, left, straight, right. If the map is wrong, it can be corrected in OpenStreetMap and the next pack picks it up.
  • The lane pick matches what you want. Both left lanes are allowed, and the routing engine prefers the right-hand one, ahead of the right turn onto Downs Dr.
  • The turns are 0.22 mi apart. On the way there is one more junction, where it also prefers the right-hand through lane.
  • The engine's own back-to-back rule did not fire here. It treats 0.22 mi as too far apart, so the app needs its own rule for when to show the following turn.

What it does

  • Shows the following turn beside the main one. Its arrow, with the distance from the first turn to the second under it.
  • Picks the lane with the next turn in mind. With two left-turn lanes and a right turn soon after, it points you to the right-hand one.
  • Says it in one sentence. "Turn left onto Peach Street, then turn right in two tenths of a mile. Use the right-hand left-turn lane."

Limits

  • Lane arrows need lane data. The map has none for the exit itself, so the first frame has no lane row. It is never guessed.
  • One junction is not proof. The lane pick worked here; it still needs checking on more junctions before it is trusted.
